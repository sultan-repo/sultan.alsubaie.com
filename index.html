Technology executive with twenty years from code to the leadership table. I set technology strategy, lead AI adoption, build the teams that own it, and run platforms for millions of customers at Saudi Arabia’s largest telecom group.

Today I lead Entertainment Technology at stc, responsible for engineering, architecture, infrastructure, operations, platform reliability and technology delivery across stc tv and its entertainment platforms.

I have seen technology leadership from both sides. I started by writing software, then moved through business analysis, digital channels, technology demand management and enterprise applications before taking on broader leadership.

A recurring theme has been building capability that lasts: in-house engineering teams, strategic platforms moved away from vendor dependency, modernized technology estates, and AI and automation that improve efficiency and operational performance.

At this level I focus on the decisions that matter most: where to build versus buy, how architecture supports business growth, how to develop strong engineering organizations, and how technology investment turns into customer and business value.

      How I lead

      Business before technologyEvery technology decision should start with the business problem, the customer need or the strategic objective it serves, never with the tool or the vendor.
      Build where ownership mattersNot everything should be built in-house, but capabilities that differentiate the business or create strategic dependency deserve ownership.
      Architecture is a business decisionArchitecture determines cost, speed, resilience and future flexibility. It is a leadership decision, not simply a technical one.
      Build teams that run without youStrong technology organizations depend on capable leaders and engineers who own their decisions, not on permanent escalation to one individual.
      Stay close to the technologyTechnical depth still matters at the leadership table. It is what lets me challenge architecture, evaluate platforms and weigh investment decisions.
      Simplify before you scaleEvery system, vendor and integration carries a long-term cost. Retiring what no longer adds value matters as much as building what’s next.
